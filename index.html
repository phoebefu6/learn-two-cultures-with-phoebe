Learn The Two Cultures with Phoebe

In 2001 Leo Breiman told statisticians there were two ways to learn from data: assume an equation generated it and read the answer off the equation, or leave nature's box shut and judge a model only by how well it predicts rows it has never seen. Four eminent statisticians replied in the same issue, and they did not agree. Twenty-five years on, every argument about explainable versus accurate, statistics versus machine learning, or "a model the regulator can read" is a descendant of that exchange. Six sessions read it at source, together with George Box on useful models, John Tukey on exploring before you confirm, and David Donoho's fifty years of data science, then put both cultures on the same 569 real biopsies in your browser and count.

The six sessions 📜 · from one black box to a choice you can defend

Two colours run through every figure and widget, so each culture always looks the same.

The data modelling culture

Assume a stochastic model generated the data, estimate its parameters, read nature's mechanism off the equation. Validated by goodness-of-fit: a yes or no.

The algorithmic culture

Treat the inside of the box as unknown, find a function that predicts y from x, and trust it only as far as its accuracy on unseen rows: a number.

Two ways to ask the data

Breiman's black box, the two cultures in his own words, the consulting projects that made him one of the 2 percent, and the hepatitis example where an unchecked model names the wrong variables. Build-along: both cultures on the same biopsies.

▶ Start here 🧭 Session 2 · advanced

All models are wrong, some are useful

Where Box actually wrote it: the idea twice in 1976, the sentence as a 1979 section heading. What "useful" means as three checks you can run, and a regression that is 99.4 percent right with coefficients that point the wrong way.

tigers, not mice 🔎 Session 3 · advanced

Explore first, confirm once

Tukey's 1962 case that data analysis is a science, exploratory work as detective work, and the discipline that keeps a confirmation honest: rows nobody touched until the end.

the untouched set ⚖️ Session 4 · bench night

The two-cultures bench

A logistic regression and a random forest fitted in your browser on 569 real biopsies. Held-out accuracy and explanation size side by side, the base rate, a break button, and a peek switch that lies.

★ the bench 🗺️ Session 5 · advanced

Fifty years of data science

Donoho's 2017 history from Tukey to today, the common task framework he calls the secret sauce of machine learning, and the six divisions of greater data science, where both cultures share one division.

the secret sauce 🤝 Session 6 · advanced

Which culture is this question?

Cox, Efron, Hoadley and Parzen in their own words, Breiman's rejoinder, and a way to choose a culture per problem that keeps their disagreement intact rather than pretending it was settled.
